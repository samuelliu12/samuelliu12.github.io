Infrared Imaging Drone

hardwarefirmware

Summary

A self-built quadcopter carrying a downward-facing FLIR Lepton thermal camera, intended for birds-eye thermal imaging of the ground below. The airframe is built around a Pixhawk 2.4.8 flight controller with GPS, four ESC-driven motors, a power distribution board, and an RC receiver, and can fly either under manual radio control or autonomously along a Mission Planner route. A Raspberry Pi 4B mounted on the frame runs the thermal camera.

What I did

  • Assembled the quadcopter frame and soldered the electronics: Pixhawk flight controller, GPS, four ESCs and motors, power distribution board, LiPo battery, buzzer, safety switch, and RC receiver.
  • Calibrated the Pixhawk (accelerometer, compass, radio, ESCs) and set up autonomous missions in Mission Planner.
  • Designed a camera holder in Onshape to mount the FLIR Lepton and Raspberry Pi under the frame, facing downward.
  • Set up the thermal imaging pipeline on the Raspberry Pi using LeptonModule to drive the FLIR Lepton, with a Qt GUI for viewing and capturing images.

Results

  • Drone flies manually with the remote control or autonomously along a Mission Planner waypoint route.
  • Thermal camera captures clear infrared images (e.g. grass vs. brick boundary), but capture is still triggered manually rather than automatically in flight.
