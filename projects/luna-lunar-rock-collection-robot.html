Luna: Lunar Rock Collection Robot

hardwarefirmwaresoftware

Summary

Luna is a rock-collection rover built in a weekend at YC RoboHacks (~10% acceptance) on top of Innate's MARS robot. The robot drives over a simulated lunar surface, spots rocks with a vision-language model and object detection pipeline, picks them up with a modified gripper, and drops them into onboard sorting bins. The picking motion itself is a learned policy rather than a scripted trajectory, trained from teleoperation demonstrations and exposed as a skill the robot's VLM agent can call.

What I did

  • Prototyped the hardware additions: grippers sized for the rock samples, sorting bins mounted on the chassis, and a force-sensitive resistor (FSR) embedded in the gripper.
  • Collected ~50 teleoperation demonstrations of rock picking and trained an ACT (Action Chunking Transformer) policy on them.
  • Deployed the trained policy as a skill for the robot's VLM agent, so the high-level planner decides what to pick and the learned policy handles how.

Results

  • Demo: given a one-line instruction, the rover scans for rocks, drives to one and attempts the pick with the learned policy, with no step-by-step control from the operator.
